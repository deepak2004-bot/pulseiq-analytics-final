\# Attendance Insights (April 2025 to September 2026)



Source: dw.fact\_attendance\_monthly, built from clean/attendance\_clean.csv. Attendance rate is present days divided by working days, using all recorded rows.



\## Finding 1: Attendance is flat over time, with no December dip



Company attendance stayed between 38.2% and 40.5% in every month from April 2025 to September 2026. December 2025 was 40.0%, in line with the rest of the year. In this data, there is no seasonal drop in December. What it means: the pattern HR may expect around year-end does not appear in these records. What HR could do: check whether year-end leave is recorded in the same way as other months before planning around a December effect.



\## Finding 2: Departments differ very widely



The lowest rates are D00 (3.5%), D06 (15.8%) and D10 (16.1%). The highest are D03 (52.6%) and D01 (50.7%). That is a gap of about 49 percentage points between the top and bottom groups. What it means: either these departments work very different patterns, or their records are coded differently. What HR could do: review the status codes for D00, D06 and D10 with the team leads before drawing any conclusion about staff behaviour.



\## Finding 3: Late arrivals are more common than leave



The data records 9,632 late arrivals and 9,016 leave days across the whole period. Lateness is a more frequent event than taking leave. What it means: punctuality may matter more to day-to-day staffing than absence does. What HR could do: look at check-in times by department and by weekday, since late arrivals are the larger signal.



\## Honest limit



This is a fictional dataset. Some departments may have very few people, so their rates can swing with one or two employees. The overall rate of about 39% is also lower than a real workplace would usually show, which suggests the status codes or the working-day definition should be checked before these numbers are used for decisions.
